Undisputed (approve as one list)

These are not fights. All three lanes (or the bind + founder law) already agree. Approving this list lets look implement them without another round. Notes still welcome. The “one exit rule” is the chrome grammar below — same idea as Instagram: you never have to guess which control leaves the screen, or whether it will abandon a check.

Today Build 18 has about six different ways out. Capture uses an X top-left. A finished result uses Done top-right. History and Settings use a left arrow. The Face primer puts Not now at the bottom. Failures have a full-width Go to Home plus a top-left arrow that also goes Home (so the arrow does not mean “back to camera”). Skin choice/primer puts Back at the bottom. Demos have Back to all checks at the top and Back under the primary. Compare has a bar arrow and Cancel. Skin review’s Back looks like retake but dumps you on Home. A person cannot learn one muscle memory. That is catalog P-SYS-02, all three families, BLOCKER.

Done

Done on covers

A cover is a full-screen overlay that is not a page in the stack: the result after a check, Ask, a failure, a sheet. There is no “back” because you didn’t walk a hallway — a card landed on you. iOS Mail compose, Photos markup, and You(th) results work this way: Done (or an X on a small data sheet) top-right, one control, nothing at the bottom. Done returns you to whoever opened it (Home after a live check; the result after Ask).

  • Face / Skin / Gut result after you just took it
  • Ask (full-screen cover from a result)
  • Failures / timeouts / camera-denied (one primary: Try again or Open Settings; Done to leave)

Chevron on pushed pages

A push is a hallway: you tapped a row and a new page slid in from the right (History → a saved check, Settings → About, All readings). iOS: chevron ‹ top-left of a locked title bar. It means “the previous page, with its scroll and filters intact.” Never a custom Back at the bottom-left. Never an X here — X would feel like destroying the list you came from.

  • History, a saved result opened from History, Trends
  • All readings, estimate detail, Skin all observations
  • Settings, Profile, About, Health connections
  • Locked title bars. The screen name stays pinned at the top while the rest of the page scrolls. Today several titles ride away with the list, so you lose “where am I?”
  • Ask from a saved result. After you tap a check in History, Ask is still there — not only on the live result you just took. Today Ask is easy to lose after Done.
  • Integers, not 62.5 or 68.00 bpm. Skin scores and whole-number vitals display as integers. Decimals stay only where the unit is truly fractional (and even then, one decimal max).
  • History rows keep the sentence. A History row says “Mostly familiar. One change.” not “HR 68 · HRV 42 · BP —”. Same “so what?” you saw on the result.
  • Opening a saved check looks like the result. Not a raw data dump. Same cards, same colours, Ask still available.
  • Apple Health has a payoff on the Health tab. Connecting it actually changes a screen (resting heart rate, sleep, a labelled series on Trends) — not a dump of permission groups that nothing else uses.
  • Two HRVs never share a line. The Face camera’s HRV and the watch’s HRV are different methods. They are not plotted as one series or one number.
  • No fake overall health score. You(th) has a composite. Sweet’s Face SDK is six vitals, not that inventory. We do not invent a grade.
  • Light only. Packet 3 already killed dark mode. The cream Home you saw in Build 15 must not come back.
  • Packet 3 look still binds. Home Codex A + C headline + B shading, Instagram pill with Settings rightmost, Vital Glimpse, Codex Skin cards, Codex Gut paint, iMessage Ask. This page is not a new look contest.

HRV on the result tile

HRV is “heart-rate variability” — how much the gap between beats changes. Higher resting HRV usually means a more flexible heart (Ranya’s sentence). There are several ways to compute it. The Face SDK (Shen) we ship does not return rMSSD. It does return SDNN (in milliseconds) and lnRMSSD (a log-scale number, no unit). Today the result still puts an rMSSD tile on the overview and hides the two we actually get. Fable’s first mocks wrote “HRV · rMSSD 42 ms” — do not approve those as drawn.

Context

The overview tile is the one number a person sees in two seconds. If that tile is rMSSD, it will be empty or invented on a real Shen check. SDNN is the honest everyday number (ms). lnRMSSD is useful for people who want the second method — it belongs on “All readings,” not the hero.

Pool rec Pool rec

Hero tile says “HRV” with a small “SDNN” under it, value in ms. All readings lists SDNN and lnRMSSD as two rows. Never mix them into one trend. Never pretend lnRMSSD is milliseconds.

Pros of the rec

Honest. Matches the vendor. Mom sees one word (HRV) and a number. People who care can still open All readings.

Cons / cost

People who googled “rMSSD” will not see that acronym on the first screen. If you insist on rMSSD as the everyday word, we would be naming a reading this SDK cannot give.
